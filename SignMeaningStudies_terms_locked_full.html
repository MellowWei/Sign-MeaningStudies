Abstract摘要 · problem / approach / contribution
English Abstract · 207 words

Traditional semiotics often treats the sign as a mediating relation: between signifier and signified, sign and object, or sign and interpretant. Such mediation-based models are powerful for describing representation, but they leave open a more elemental question: what allows a sign to become discipline-generating force rather than merely a representational unit? Sign-Meaning Studies answers by suspending the mediation frame and redefining the sign as a meaningful, ren-yi-carrying force-event. The framework is founded on two axioms: SE-0 positions Sign-Meaning Studies as the father-discipline of future modern disciplines; SE-1 defines its object as the sign itself, understood as force that carries both meaning and ren-yi. The contribution is threefold: conceptually, it proposes a force-ontological account of signs; methodologically, it connects sign-formation to SourceFields, MetaLogic, Real Physics, and Real Ethics without subordinating to them; and architecturally, it provides a generative layer through which future disciplines may name their own objects. This is a philosophical research artifact, not a completed empirical theory, not an institutional claim over existing disciplines, and not a refutation of Saussure or Peirce within their own frames.
